Consulting services

Airport consulting

Planning, simulation and technical support for airports and engineering teams.

Project applications

Typical projects include new airports and terminals, expansion, refurbishment during live operations, airline or facility relocation, phased investment and operational improvement.

01Airline relocation

Assess whether check-in, security and transfer facilities can accommodate relocated airlines, and compare suitable layouts.

02New terminals and airport expansion

Compare opening dates, facility size and the division of functions between new and existing terminals.

03Airside layout changes

Assess how revised taxiways and stands affect aircraft movements and ground-handling vehicle flows.

04Refurbishment during live operations

Assess which facilities can close together and compare temporary routes and construction sequences.

05Joint design and engineering proposals

Join proposals as a technical partner for simulation and capacity assessment, then undertake the agreed analysis after award.

06Airport development strategy

Assess demand, capacity and alternatives to support investment timing and facility sizing.

07National multi-airport master planning

Develop national master plans covering multiple airports, assessing airport roles, demand allocation, capacity and phased development priorities.

08Preliminary studies for airport expansion and new airports

Undertake preliminary studies for individual airport expansions and new-airport projects, assessing demand, facility requirements and development alternatives.

Translate future demand into operating scenarios, facility requirements and expansion decisions.

01Future traffic and passenger demand

Develop target-year and growth-stage scenarios from existing forecasts and operating data to assess facility and operating requirements.

02Design-day and peak-hour demand

Translate annual demand into representative-day, peak-day and hourly profiles to establish the loads facilities must accommodate.

03Future flight schedule generation

Develop design-day schedules from demand, fleet mix, airline patterns and operating hours, then assess their operational feasibility.

04Fleet mix and airline patterns

Compare how aircraft size, airline and route mix, and arrival and departure banks affect gate, stand and terminal demand.

05Passenger segments and arrival profiles

Model when and where departing, arriving and transfer passengers reach facilities, accounting for show-up patterns and gate allocations.

06Capacity gaps and expansion triggers

Test rising demand against capacity limits and remaining headroom to recommend expansion thresholds, indicative timing and facility priorities.

Assess passenger processing, facility layouts, airline relocation, construction phasing and access arrangements.

07Terminal facility sizing and adequacy

Assess processing facilities and waiting areas against demand and service criteria to identify required facility quantities and operating hours.

08Departure processes and operating plans

Compare facility opening, operating hours and staffing across check-in, self bag drop, boarding-pass checks, security and departure immigration.

09Arrivals, baggage reclaim and customs

Model arrival immigration, baggage reclaim and customs together to assess how changes at one stage affect total processing time and downstream congestion.

10Transfer flows and terminal connections

Assess transfer security, gate-to-gate movement, corridors and inter-terminal transit platforms for congestion and connection times.

11Airline relocation and facility impacts

Compare airline moves between terminals, concourses, check-in zones or gates for passenger distribution, facility loads, transfer routes and gate lounge congestion.

12New terminals and phased expansion

Compare facility size, layout, roles and connections with existing terminals to assess capacity at opening and later expansion stages.

13Refurbishment during live airport operations

Assess closures and reduced facility availability to determine workable closure combinations and temporary operating arrangements.

14Passenger circulation and queue layouts

Compare corridor widths, partitions, diversions and queue layouts for walking distance, congestion and dwell time, then recommend spatial improvements.

15Landside access and transport interfaces

Assess passenger and vehicle arrivals and layout options for terminal curbs, parking, access roads and public transport interfaces within the agreed scope.

16Combined conditions and stress scenarios

Combine peak demand, flight delays, closures and airline relocation to identify vulnerable areas and compare congestion relief and recovery options.

Evaluate runway, taxiway, stand and support-facility plans alongside aircraft and ground-handling operations.

17Runway capacity and flight delays

Model operating modes, occupancy, separation and concentrated traffic to assess runway throughput and arrival and departure delays, including low-visibility conditions.

18Rapid-exit taxiway locations and layouts

Compare rapid-exit taxiway positions and spacing using aircraft landing characteristics and runway occupancy considerations.

19Airside layout changes and impacts

Assess how new, extended, reconnected or closed runways, taxiways and aprons affect routes, taxi times, bottlenecks and operational interference.

20Stand allocation and simultaneous pushback

Assess aircraft compatibility, contact and remote stand allocation, occupancy and simultaneous movements to recommend layout and operating alternatives.

21Aircraft and ground-handling vehicle movements

Model aircraft movement and pushback alongside vehicle tasks and routes to assess service-road congestion, crossing interference and vehicle operating plans.

22Cargo aprons and freighter operations

Assess large-freighter stand compatibility, occupancy, access routes and connections with cargo terminals and ground logistics.

23MRO, hangar and GA/FBO access

Review aircraft compatibility, hangar access, towing routes and interactions with adjacent aprons and service roads.

24De-icing facility layouts and operations

Assess simultaneous accommodation, access and exit routes, and aircraft clearances, then compare queues and departure delays under variable processing times.

25ARFF, fuel and support-facility siting

Assess rescue and firefighting, fuel and utility facility locations, access routes and service coverage to support layout and operating decisions.

26Tower visibility and obstacle constraints

Compare sightlines and blind spots, and review obstacle limitation surface overlaps associated with runway and surrounding facility plans at the planning stage.

Model airport development stages, validate operating rules and build the analysis capabilities required for the project.

27Current and future airport models

Prepare spatial inputs and model existing, opening, interim expansion and ultimate layouts with the facilities planned for each stage.

28Airport-specific models and operational validation

Configure distinctive layouts, facility-use rules and operating procedures, then validate and calibrate against available observations and operating records.

29Design overlays and spatial rendering

Compare existing plans, alternatives and construction phases, and explain aircraft, vehicle and passenger flows through plan views, spatial models and simulation videos.

30Custom KPIs and analysis features

Define airport-specific measures, including simultaneous gate operations and transfer-time exceedances, and configure the required data inputs, outputs and result-access functions.

Scope, model detail and assessment criteria are agreed against your airport data, operating conditions and project requirements. Additional studies, feature development and external-system integration are scoped with the required schedule.

Technology and airport experience

Team Flexa grew out of Incheon International Airport Corporation’s in-house venture programme. Our team has worked on operations improvement and expansion at Incheon Airport, as well as assignments within the corporation’s overseas airport business.

In-house software

We develop and own our software, adapting models and functions to the study while reducing the need for separate analysis tools or specialist staff.

Custom KPIs and analysis features

We use your performance measures, assessment criteria and reporting formats, agreeing additional development or integration where existing functions are insufficient.

Airport-specific rules and layouts

We model terminal connections, airline facility-use rules, stand configurations, ground-handling routes and time-dependent restrictions.

Existing data and models

We use available airport, flight and facility data and existing models to reduce setup time, validating against your observations and agreed criteria.

Linked aircraft, vehicle and passenger flows

Aircraft movements and vehicle tasks are modelled together; gate allocations and passenger arrivals link airside and terminal studies to assess the wider effects of facility changes.

Consulting with TAAM, CAST and other solutions

Consulting can also be delivered using TAAM, CAST and other simulation tools alongside our solutions, according to the study scope and project requirements.
